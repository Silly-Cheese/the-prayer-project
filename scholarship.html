Who may become a Beacon Scholar

You are more than a number.

Proposed criteria focus on potential, honest reflection, and character. A 2.5 minimum opens the door for students who may not have perfect grades but have something meaningful to say.

Proposed applicant criteria

  • Students currently in grades 9 through 12. Freshmen, sophomores, juniors, and seniors are eligible to be considered under this proposal; students already beyond grade 12 are not included.
  • At least a 2.5 GPA on a 4.0 scale, or a documented school-certified equivalent. A higher GPA would not automatically outrank an applicant with a 2.5.
  • One meaningful original personal essay and two separate references: one academic and one character reference.
  • A clear educational goal and a brief explanation of financial barriers.
  • Enrollment and GPA documentation, requested through a secure process only when needed for final verification; a current ninth grader would use the most recent available school-reported GPA.

What applicants do not need

  • Community service is not required. Volunteering can illustrate kindness or initiative, but jobs, caregiving, school participation, and other responsibilities can do the same.
  • No perfect grades, major awards, leadership titles, or expensive extracurricular activities are expected.
  • No church membership, religious profession, donation, or volunteer affiliation with The Prayer Project is required under the proposed approach.
  • No personal trauma disclosures or polished, extraordinary life story are necessary; honesty and reflection matter more.
  • Geographic reach, guardian consent for minors, precise school-equivalency rules, and permitted educational expenses still need formal approval before launch.
Recognition selection process · Planned

Every student deserves to be heard.

The first applications would be for recognition only. An eligible student could be selected as a Beacon Scholar and receive a letter, but not scholarship funds. Any future financial-award application would be announced separately.

01

Basic application

Provide current grade level (9–12), high school, a 2.5-or-higher GPA or equivalent, educational goals, and appropriate contact details through a future secure portal.

02

Personal essay

Tell a specific, meaningful story in your own words (approximately 500–750 words). Explain why it matters, not just what happened.

03

Two references

Identify an academic and a character reference. A financial-need statement is not required for recognition; it may be relevant only to a later funded award program.

04

Committee review

The Beacon Scholars Selection Committee would consider the essay, both references, character, and educational goals before making honorary recognition decisions.

05

Official letter

Selected students would receive their Beacon Scholar recognition letter by email or postal mail, with no monetary award attached.

No application form, account registration, uploads, or deadlines are active. Students should not send essays, references, financial records, transcripts, or identity documents to The Prayer Project at this stage.

Two required recommendations

Two people who know you in different ways.

The references would be supplied by two different people. Applicants would ask each person's permission before providing contact details or requesting a future letter.

01

Academic reference

A teacher, guidance counselor, academic adviser, principal, or instructor who knows your work, effort, growth, or readiness to learn.

Suggested focus: dedication, improvement, curiosity, effort, and how you handle challenges in school—not just your GPA.

02

Character reference

A coach, mentor, employer, community leader, clergy member, or another unrelated adult who can speak to your honesty, reliability, kindness, or perseverance.

Suggested focus: who you are beyond grades, including everyday responsibilities. Community service may be mentioned, but is not expected.

Family members should not serve as references, and one person cannot fill both roles. If the program opens, recommendations would be collected privately and securely under published privacy and consent rules; no reference is being requested or contacted now.

The heart of the application

We want to know the person behind the application.

We are not looking for the most impressive list of achievements. We want a story that matters to you, what you felt and learned, and what receiving this opportunity would mean in your own life.

Illustrative recognition essay · 500–750 words

Who are you beyond your grades?

“Think about a moment in your life that has stayed with you. It could be something someone said to you, a responsibility you carry, a mistake you learned from, a kindness you will never forget, or a small moment that changed how you see yourself. Tell us what happened, how it made you feel, and why it still matters to you. What would you want our Scholarship Selection Committee to understand about the person you are today? Finally, tell us—in your own words—what it would mean to you to be recognized as a Beacon Scholar, and what you hope for the future you are building.”

Your answer does not need to be dramatic or sad. A quiet, ordinary memory can mean more than a long list of accomplishments. Write about something you are comfortable sharing and genuinely care about. The official prompt and word count will be confirmed before applications open.

Let the committee get to know you
  • Choose one real moment or experience rather than summarizing your whole life
  • Describe what you felt and thought, not just what happened
  • Explain why that moment matters to you personally
  • Show something about your values, growth, or hopes
  • Say what being recognized as a Beacon Scholar would mean to you

This is not a contest for the hardest life story. An honest, ordinary experience is enough. Students should write their own essays; future guidance will explain permitted editing help. No religious belief, trauma, private medical history, or service hours are required.

Planned decision-making body

Beacon Scholars Selection Committee

A proposed committee of three to five appointed members would select recognition recipients and authorize official letters. Recognition does not depend on a scholarship budget. No committee has been appointed yet.

01 / Review

Independent reading

Eligible essays, academic and character references, and educational goals would be reviewed against clear recognition criteria.

02 / Discuss

Consider the whole student

Members discuss the strength and meaning of each application without treating GPA above 2.5 or optional volunteer hours as automatic advantages.

03 / Decide

Committee vote

The committee would vote on who is recognized as a Beacon Scholar under adopted quorum, voting, and conflict-of-interest rules. No money would be awarded.

04 / Record

Document & communicate

Selections and recusals would be documented privately, and selected students would receive their recognition letters by verified email or chosen postal mail.

Independence and fairness: Conflicted members would recuse themselves. Donors would not choose recipients, and private essays, reference letters, email addresses, and postal addresses would not be published. The board would establish appointment, privacy, review, voting, correction-of-error, and minor-consent procedures before recognition selections begin.

An announced period for applicants to prepare and submit an essay and the other required materials.

Committee review: roughly 2–3 weeks

Eligibility screening, independent reading, committee discussion, and follow-up for finalists when needed.

Recognition & notification: roughly 1–2 weeks

Verify grades 9–12 and GPA, finalize committee selections, and prepare letters for email or postal delivery.

These are possible durations for an initial recognition cycle, not a published schedule or guarantee of letter delivery. No application window or committee start date has been announced. Funded scholarship applications would be a separate future program.

Building the Beacon Fund · After BWR

Together, we hope to make more light possible.

After Bibles Within Reach has concluded, we intend to raise as much support as responsibly possible for Beacon Scholars through a dedicated effort involving individuals, churches, businesses, and community partners. More funding could mean more recipients or larger awards—but neither is guaranteed.

Possible donor pathways

  • Beacon Scholarship Fund: Contributions from a future separate campaign pooled for committee-selected student awards, subject to approval and financial safeguards.
  • Sponsored awards: Possible donor-supported award categories under the program's published, independent eligibility standards.
  • Community partnerships: Schools, churches, and organizations sharing approved scholarship information with eligible students.
  • Recognition preferences: A future option to recognize supporters or respect donor anonymity, consistent with privacy and organizational policy.

Safeguards before accepting support

  • Approve a written gift-acceptance and restricted-fund policy.
  • Keep donor decisions separate from applicant scoring and selection.
  • Use documented financial controls, expense tracking, and appropriate scholarship disbursement methods.
  • Publish truthful funding and award updates without sharing private applicant stories without consent.
  • Explain any tax treatment only after the organization's status and applicable requirements have been verified.

Beacon fundraising has not begun. Bibles Within Reach remains a separate initiative; restricted BWR donations would not be redirected to Beacon. This page does not collect gifts, and no donor tiers, tax benefits, funding totals, or award cycles are promised. A separate, authorized Beacon fundraiser could begin after BWR is complete and required policies are approved.

Common questions

What is known—and what is not yet.

We would rather state what's still undecided than create expectations we cannot meet.

Can I become a Beacon Scholar right now?

Not yet. Beacon Scholars is a future initiative, with no active application portal, award cycle, deadlines, or recipient selection.

What grades can apply, and what GPA is needed?

The proposed program covers current high school students in grades 9–12, with a minimum GPA of 2.5 on a 4.0 scale or a verified equivalent. Grade 13 and students already beyond grade 12 are not included. Details for nonstandard grading scales will be published before launch.

How personal should my essay be?

Very personal in its meaning, but only as private as you are comfortable making it. Choose a real moment, describe how it affected you, and explain what you want the committee to understand about who you are. The proposed 500–750-word essay should also explain what receiving this scholarship could mean. You do not need to share trauma or sensitive details.

What two references do I need?

Two different people: one academic reference, such as a teacher or counselor, and one character reference, such as a coach, employer, or mentor. They should not be relatives. The future application would explain how to submit recommendations securely.

Do I have to complete community service?

No. Community service can be meaningful, but it is not required and applicants without volunteer hours would not be penalized. Everyday service to family, school, friends, or community can demonstrate character in many ways.

Will Beacon Scholars receive $500–$10,000?

That is only the speculative individual award range we hope might be possible if fundraising succeeds. Actual amounts, award types, and the number of recipients have not been approved or funded; we might make smaller awards, different awards, or none at all.

Will I need to be Christian to receive an award?

The proposed design does not require affiliation with a church or a particular faith. Final eligibility criteria will be adopted and published before any application opens.

Do I have to disclose my family income?

The program is considering an initial written explanation of financial barriers, followed by only proportionate verification if warranted. No financial documentation is being collected now.

Who decides who becomes a Beacon Scholar?

The proposed Beacon Scholarship Selection Committee would read eligible applications, consider essays and references, and vote on recipients and award amounts under board-approved policies. The committee has not yet been formed. Donors would not choose recipients, and conflicts of interest would require recusal.

Can a donor influence the committee's decision?

No. The proposed safeguards keep donors out of individual selection decisions. Gifts would not purchase committee votes, preference for a student, or access to confidential essays and references.

When would Beacon fundraising and applications begin?

Fundraising for Beacon Scholars is intended to begin only after Bibles Within Reach has concluded and necessary authorization is in place. Applications would open later, if sufficient funding and the program's legal, financial, privacy, committee, and disbursement rules are approved. No dates have been set.

How would scholarship funds be delivered?

Final payment rules remain undecided. A possible safeguard is paying eligible educational expenses directly to the institution or verified vendor where appropriate, rather than promising unrestricted cash.

No active application or donation window
