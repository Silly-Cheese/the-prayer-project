Why Partnerships Matter

The people closest to a need often understand it best.

A good initiative should not arrive in a community assuming it already knows everything.

Partnership is how BWR listens before it acts.

A church may know families quietly struggling. A shelter may understand what people experiencing homelessness actually need. A community organization may already have trusted relationships we could never build overnight. A supplier may know how to make each dollar stretch farther. A business may be able to solve a practical problem we have not even encountered yet.

That is why partnership is not an afterthought for Bibles Within Reach. It is part of the plan from the beginning.

We are not looking for organizations to simply lend us a logo. We are looking for honest, useful relationships where each side understands its role, the people being served are treated with dignity, and the work becomes stronger because it is being done together.

“For we are labourers together with God.”1 Corinthians 3:9
Ways to Partner

You do not have to do everything to make a difference.

Some organizations may fit one role. Others may fit several. We want partnership to be practical, not performative, and flexible enough to reflect what each organization already does well.

01

Distribution Partners

Churches, ministries, shelters, outreach organizations, and other trusted groups may help place Bibles with people they already know and serve.

Potential role: identify appropriate needs, receive approved Bible quantities, distribute voluntarily and respectfully, and submit simple distribution reports.
02

Community & Referral Partners

Not every partner needs to distribute Bibles. Some may simply help us understand local needs, connect us with the right people, or point us toward communities where access is limited.

Potential role: local insight, introductions, referrals, outreach coordination, or helping us understand where BWR could serve responsibly.
03

Business & Supplier Partners

Businesses, retailers, publishers, vendors, and logistics partners may help with Bible sourcing, pricing, purchasing, storage, transport, packaging, or other practical needs.

Potential role: preferred pricing, in-kind support, supplier coordination, transportation, operational support, or other mission-aligned assistance.
04

Outreach & Event Partners

Some partners may be able to serve alongside The Prayer Project during direct outreach or help create appropriate opportunities where Bibles can be offered to people who want them.

Potential role: outreach locations, volunteers, local coordination, event support, or helping create safe and respectful distribution environments.

What a Distribution Report Will Capture

Enough information to account for Bibles. Not enough to invade someone's privacy.

A partner distribution report should be quick to complete and useful to BWR. The goal is to know what happened to initiative inventory without turning recipients into records.

QuantityBibles received, distributed during the report, and remaining.
ContextGeneral date or period, service area, and distribution setting.
No recipient listNo names or identifying information about people receiving Bibles.

Partners may also be able to add optional notes about needs, future requests, or lessons learned. Public reporting would use aggregate totals rather than exposing partner or recipient details unnecessarily.

What We Ask of Partners

Simple expectations. Clear purpose.

Respect

Protect the dignity of recipients.

No person should be pressured to receive a Bible, treated like campaign content, or required to participate in another activity as a condition of receiving one.

Stewardship

Use resources for the agreed purpose.

Bibles and initiative resources should be handled carefully, distributed appropriately, and not diverted into unrelated uses.

Reporting

Close the loop.

Distribution partners should report what was distributed and what remains so BWR's inventory and impact totals reflect what actually happened.
